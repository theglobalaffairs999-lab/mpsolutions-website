Who rules Andhra Pradesh? Every government since 1983

Ten Assembly elections, three parties in power, and what the 2024 result means for the state.

Home › Articles › Who rules Andhra Pradesh

By MP Solutions Election Desk · Published 15 November 2026 · Explainer

Since 1983, Andhra Pradesh has held ten Assembly elections. The ruling party lost seven of them. Three parties have governed the state: the Telugu Desam Party (TDP), the Congress and the YSR Congress Party (YSRCP). Each time the verdict has been decisive, and the seat count has rarely been close.

Who governs now

In the 2024 election the TDP, Jana Sena and BJP fought together. Jana Sena won all 21 seats it contested and the BJP won 8 of 10, giving the NDA 164 seats. The TDP alone won 135 seats, and the YSRCP fell to 11, short of the 18 needed for official opposition status.

The 2024 result on the map

The orange areas show where the NDA alliance won. Most of the state is orange, which matches the scale of the 2024 verdict. The blue seats are scattered in clusters across the state, mostly in the west, the south and parts of the north.

Why the government changes

  • Ruling parties usually lose. They held on only three times: 1985, 1999 and 2009.
  • Waves, not close calls. Most elections are decided by a statewide swing, so local work in a few seats rarely changes the result.
  • Alliances decide close fights. The TDP won when it had allies, and lost when the opposition vote was united against it.
  • Third forces split votes. Praja Rajyam won only 18 seats in 2009 but took 16% of the vote, which helped the Congress hold on.
  • Long walks matter. Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy in 2004 and Jagan Mohan Reddy in 2019 built their wins on long statewide padayatras.
