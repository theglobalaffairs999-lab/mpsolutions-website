Punjab Assembly Election 2027: BJP–SAD Alliance Returns — Can the Opposition Dislodge AAP?

What the 2022 and 2024 results, a November 2026 opinion poll and the new alliance do and do not tell us about Punjab's next Assembly election.

The big development: BJP and Shiromani Akali Dal reunite

The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) and the Shiromani Akali Dal (SAD) announced on 24 November 2026 that they will contest the 2027 Punjab Assembly election together, ending a six-year separation. The announcement was made at the BJP headquarters in New Delhi, on Guru Purab, by Union Home Minister Amit Shah, BJP president Nitin Nabin and SAD president Sukhbir Singh Badal.

The split came in September 2020, when SAD left the BJP-led National Democratic Alliance over the Centre's three farm laws, which were later repealed. As recently as March 2026, Shah had said the BJP would fight the 2027 Punjab election on its own strength. The reunion restores an old partnership, but it does not by itself show how voters will respond or how votes will move between the two parties.

According to reports of the press conference, Shah said SAD would be the "big brother" in the alliance and that the Chief Minister would come from the Akali Dal. Reports say the parties will contest all 117 seats between them, but no constituency-wise seat-sharing formula was announced. Some reports, citing unnamed sources, say the BJP may contest around 40 seats. That figure is a reported negotiating possibility, not a confirmed or signed arrangement.

Why it matters: SAD brings an established regional organisation and a historical rural network; the BJP brings a separate vote base and a national organisation. The outcome will depend on whether those electorates transfer to a common candidate, whether local workers cooperate, and how voters view the reconciliation after the farm-law dispute.

Assembly election 2022: the baseline

AAP won 92 of Punjab's 117 seats in 2022, with 42.01% of the vote. Congress won 18 seats with 22.98%; SAD won three with 18.38%; the BJP won two with 6.60%. The BSP and one independent candidate won one seat each.

A caution on alliance arithmetic. SAD's 18.38% and the BJP's 6.60% in 2022 add up to 24.98%. That is only a historical reference, not a 2027 alliance vote-share forecast. The parties contested different constituencies, and supporters may not transfer uniformly when they share candidates.

Lok Sabha election 2024: a second electoral signal

The 2024 parliamentary election in Punjab was far more evenly split. Congress won seven of the state's 13 seats, AAP three and SAD one, and two independents also won. The BJP won no seat but took 18.56% of the vote, up from 6.60% in the 2022 Assembly election. Congress finished 0.28 percentage points ahead of AAP.

Election Commission data, as reported by PTI, showed AAP leading in only 33 of the 117 Assembly segments in the 2024 Lok Sabha contest. A parliamentary vote is not interchangeable with an Assembly vote: candidates, alliances, local issues and turnout all differ. It does show, though, that the BJP's statewide share in 2024 was materially higher than in 2022, and that the 2022 map should not be assumed to repeat.

For reference only, the 2024 shares of the BJP (18.56%) and SAD (13.42%) add up to 31.98%. Like the 2022 sum above, this is arithmetic, not a forecast.

What voters say are the biggest issues

Tracker 2 asked respondents to name the most important election issue. The leading answers were drug abuse (31.1%), unemployment and migration (23.0%) and law and order and women's safety (17.3%).

These are survey findings, not independently verified measures of how widespread those problems are.

The three-region lens: Malwa, Majha and Doaba

Political commentary commonly divides Punjab into three broad regions. These are analytical and geographic groupings, not official Election Commission divisions.

Malwa alone has 69 seats, more than half the Assembly, and AAP won 66 of them in 2022. A Scroll.in analysis of the 2022 result found Doaba to be the one sub-region where the contest between Congress and AAP stayed close. Lok Sabha segment leads are not Assembly results, but the 2024 pattern is a warning against assuming the 2022 map will repeat.

Five questions that will shape the alliance's prospects

1. Can the BJP and SAD transfer votes to each other?

The two parties must turn separate historical support bases into coordinated constituency-level votes. A statewide addition of old vote shares is not enough. Candidate choice, local rivalries, campaign messaging and turnout can all disrupt transfers.

2. Can SAD rebuild trust after the 2020 split?

The farm-law dispute was the central reason for the breakup. The alliance will need to explain the reunion to voters, farmers and party workers. The reaction of farmer organisations should be tracked through public statements and constituency reporting, not assumed.

3. How will AAP defend its 2022 mandate?

AAP's 92-seat victory sets a high benchmark. Its campaign will be judged on governance, local MLA performance, public services, employment, drugs, law and order and the delivery of commitments.

4. Can Congress turn its 2024 performance into Assembly strength?

Congress won seven Lok Sabha seats in 2024 with a 26.30% vote share. Its challenge is to convert parliamentary support into a coherent Assembly campaign, manage leadership and candidate selection, and compete across all three regions.

5. What will the final seat-sharing formula be?

The 24 November announcement did not publish a constituency-by-constituency allocation. Reports of the BJP contesting around 40 seats remain unconfirmed until the parties formally announce them. The final allocation, candidate list and local arrangements will be major signals.

What can be concluded today?

The BJP–SAD reunion is a consequential change in Punjab's pre-election alignment. It gives the opposition a new structure to test, but it does not show that AAP has been defeated or that the alliance has a fixed vote bank. The 2022 result, the 2024 parliamentary election and the 12 November poll all point to a competitive, regionally varied contest.

The strongest evidence-based conclusion is that the 2027 election is open to significant realignment, but a reliable seat forecast is premature. The next useful step is constituency-level work: candidate strength, prior margins, 2024 Assembly-segment leads, local issues and credible post-alliance polling.
