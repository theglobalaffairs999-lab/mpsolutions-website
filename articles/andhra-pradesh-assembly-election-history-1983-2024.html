Andhra Pradesh election history, 1983–2024

Ten Assembly elections, seven defeats for the ruling party, and the patterns behind every verdict.

Home › Articles › AP election history

By MP Solutions Election Desk · Published 13 November 2026 · Explainer

Since 1983, Andhra Pradesh has held ten Assembly elections, and the ruling party lost seven of them. Three parties have ruled: the Telugu Desam Party (TDP), the Congress, and the YSR Congress Party (YSRCP). The state's voters have a habit of giving big, decisive verdicts. Only once, in 2009, did the winner take less than 55% of the seats.

The size of each mandate

The chart shows how one-sided most verdicts have been. Even the closest result, 2009, gave the Congress a majority on its own. After bifurcation the mandates grew sharper still: the YSRCP's 86% of seats in 2019 was followed by the TDP winning 77% of seats on its own in 2024.

The story, election by election

  • 1983 – NTR's wave. Film star N.T. Rama Rao founded the TDP in March 1982 on the slogan of Telugu self-respect. Within nine months it won 201 of 294 seats, ending Congress rule that had lasted since the state was formed. NTR became the first non-Congress Chief Minister.
  • 1984–85 – Crisis and a bigger mandate. In August 1984 NTR was briefly replaced by N. Bhaskara Rao; he was restored within a month after his MLAs were paraded before the President. NTR then called an early election in 1985 and won 202 seats.
  • 1989 – Congress comes back. The Congress won 181 seats, but rotated three Chief Ministers in five years.
  • 1994 – NTR returns, then Naidu. The TDP won 216 seats; the Congress fell to 26. In September 1995 N. Chandrababu Naidu took over the party and the government from NTR.
  • 1999 – Naidu's first own mandate. Contesting with the BJP, the TDP won 180 seats, campaigning on Naidu's development record.
  • 2004 – The YSR wave. After a 1,400 km padayatra across the state, Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy led the Congress to 185 seats. The TDP fell to 47.
  • 2009 – The three-way contest. Chiranjeevi's new Praja Rajyam Party took 16.32% of the vote and 18 seats, splitting the anti-Congress vote. The Congress held on with 156. YSR died in a helicopter crash in September 2009; K. Rosaiah and then N. Kiran Kumar Reddy followed.
  • 2014 – A new state, a new map. Andhra Pradesh was bifurcated and Telangana formed on 2 June 2014. In the residuary state's 175 seats, the TDP (with the BJP, and backed by Pawan Kalyan) won 102 against the YSRCP's 67. The Congress, blamed for the division, won none.
  • 2019 – Jagan's landslide. After his own padayatra, Y.S. Jagan Mohan Reddy led the YSRCP to 151 seats and 49.95% of the vote. The TDP won 23; Jana Sena won 1.
  • 2024 – The alliance sweep. The TDP, Jana Sena and BJP fought together. Jana Sena won all 21 seats it contested and the BJP 8 of 10, giving the NDA 164 seats. The YSRCP fell to 11, short of the 18 needed for official opposition status.

Four patterns in AP's voting

Ruling parties held on only three times: 1985, 1999 and 2009.

  1. Waves, not close calls. Most elections are decided by a statewide swing. That is why booth-level work in a handful of seats rarely saves a party on the wrong side of a wave.
  2. Alliances decide close fights. The TDP won when it had allies (BJP in 1999 and 2014; Jana Sena and BJP in 2024) and lost when the opposition vote was united against it.
  3. Third forces split votes. Praja Rajyam in 2009 won only 18 seats but took 16% of the vote, which helped the Congress retain power.
  4. Padayatras matter. Both YSR (2004) and Jagan (2019) built their wins on long statewide walks. Naidu's 2012–13 padayatra came before his 2014 return too.

Exam corner: quick facts for APPSC and UPSC

  • First non-Congress CM of Andhra Pradesh: N.T. Rama Rao (January 1983).
  • TDP founded: 29 March 1982, by N.T. Rama Rao.
  • Assembly size: 294 seats in united Andhra Pradesh; 175 seats since 2014 (Telangana: 119).
  • Telangana formed: 2 June 2014, under the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014.
  • First CM of residuary Andhra Pradesh: N. Chandrababu Naidu (June 2014).
  • Majority mark today: 88 of 175. Official opposition status needs 18 seats (one-tenth of the House).
  • Highest single-party tally since 2014: YSRCP, 151 seats (2019).
